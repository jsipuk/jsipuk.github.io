Invent Together

What should we make?

Use this while talking together. The child supplies the ideas. The grown-up types. There are no wrong answers.

Nothing answered yet

1. Who is inventing?

Optional, but it puts their name on the poster at the end.

First name is plenty.

2. Pick a starting direction

Read these out. Let them press the one they want.

Write their answer as closely as you can. Their words, not tidied up.

3. The first thing in the world

Ask them to draw it on paper first if you can.

The exact name, however odd.

Creature, robot, person, object…

Colours, shape, number of legs, wheels, eyes, size.

Powers, skills, funny behaviour, surprising facts.

Forest, moon, bedroom, underwater city…

4. What do you actually do?

This turns a character into something you can play.

Collect things, solve puzzles, build, explore, race, count.

Reach somewhere, solve something, make something, help someone.

Obstacles, puzzles, choices, limited items, silly mistakes.

Animation, new area, badge, funny sound, new creation.

5. Make it feel like theirs

The place, the mood, and the things in it.

Let them make the noises. Write them down exactly.

The one thing you must not forget.

6. Learning, if it fits naturally

Optional. Do not force an educational mechanic if it makes the idea less fun.

For example: the creature needs 7 berries and has 4.

7. Grown-up notes

Things said along the way that do not fit anywhere else. These are often the best bits.

Word for word. One of these goes on the poster.

Extra ideas, observations, drawings made, things they got excited about.

Keep the first build tiny. One idea, one screen, one interaction is enough.

8. Make the invention

Two things come out of this: a poster for them, and a brief for you.
