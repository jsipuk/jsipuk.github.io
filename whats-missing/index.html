Research note · August 2026

You keep designing the system.
You never build the substrate.

Sixteen things live on this site. Every one of them is excellent, self contained, and forgets everything the moment you close the tab. That is the gap. Not another app. The layer underneath the ones you already have.

01

What the evidence actually says

I read every project in the repository, every installed skill, and the reports you wrote about your own job. The same shape appears in all of them.

You wrote the roadmap and built none of it The Future SE Stack ranks 22 systems by leverage and names five as buildable now. Not one exists. The five all depend on the same missing thing: a store of your own judgement to draw from.
Champion Deposits is a complete operating model with nowhere to live Source framework, 25 topic tags, a contact schema, confidence scoring, frequency rules. The README says it plainly: The spreadsheet doesn't exist yet.
Five versions of one skill, each hand maintained inbox-sweep through inbox-sweep-v5. Every new sender means a new copy of the whole map. Knowledge that should sit in data is sitting in prompt text.
Your POV tracker is designed so nothing accumulates The skill instructs a unique storageKey per plan “so browser progress from different plans never collides”. Correct for progress. It also means sixty scenarios of hard-won knowledge are sealed off the day the POV ends.
The positioning skill starts from zero every time netskope-ai-positioning re-researches, re-verifies and re-derives on every run. Nothing it establishes on Monday is available to it on Friday.

You have built sixteen roofs and no foundations. Each one is fine. None of them holds anything up for the next one.

02

The thing that is missing

A durable, private, portable store of your own professional judgement, built from the start to be handed to an AI as context.

Every system in your own report needs it. The RFP engine needs an answer library. The discovery engine needs to know what you already learned about the account. The competitive agent needs what you actually heard in the room, not what a vendor page says. Champion Deposits needs the contact sheet. You cannot build any of them on top of nothing, which is exactly why you have not.

Two things make this urgent rather than nice

Your expertise accrues to someone else

  • Vivun, Tribble, SiftHub, AutoRFP, Consensus: every commercial equivalent is cloud SaaS bought by the employer
  • What you learn lives in their tenant
  • You cannot buy one personally, and you do not leave with it
  • Five years of judgement, and none of it is yours to take

You already know practice works

  • You built Aria for piano
  • Then you built Encore for piano as well
  • Two deliberate practice tools for a hobby
  • Zero for the skill that pays the mortgage

Everything below is offline, local first, no account, no network call. That is not a constraint I imposed. It is the standard every other thing on this site already meets, and it is the only way the store stays yours.

03

Three ways to fill it

These are not three skins of one idea. They are three different daily rituals, and they will suit you differently depending on when you actually have a spare two minutes. All three are working software, not mockups. Try each for ten minutes.

V1

Field Notes

The library. A notebook you empty your head into.

One box, one keystroke to save. Capture the objection, the sentence that landed, the question that opened something up, the limitation you found the hard way. Tag inline with #dlp and @Northbank. Then find any of it again in a second, and export a filtered slice as a context pack for Claude.

When: after a call Cost: 3 to 5 min Pays back: in weeks
Strongest at
  • Lowest possible friction to capture
  • Becomes the RFP answer library from your own report
  • Works across every account at once
  • Resurfaces old notes so the library stays alive
Honest risk
  • Capture tools die when nobody captures
  • Value is nearly zero for the first fortnight
  • Needs a habit you do not currently have
Open Field Notes
V2

The Dojo

The gym. Encore, but for the thing that pays.

A rep is ninety seconds. One real question under a clock: a CFO asking what they stop paying for, an architect telling you inline inspection will break their applications, a customer asking privately whether they should actually buy it. You answer, then you see the shape of a good answer rather than a model answer, mark yourself on clarity, evidence and control, and the ones you fumble come back on Thursday. Answers you keep become the library as a by-product.

When: dead time, before a call Cost: 90 sec Pays back: immediately
Strongest at
  • Useful on day one with zero setup, unlike the other two
  • 44 seeded cards, all vendor neutral craft
  • Spaced repetition on your weakest categories
  • Builds the library without asking you to keep a diary
Honest risk
  • Self marking is only as honest as you are
  • Feels like homework on a bad week
  • Deck needs your own cards added over time
Open The Dojo
V3

Account Brain

The field. One living page per account.

People and where they stand, what is still open against you, and above all what you promised and have not sent. Press one button before a call and get a brief you can read in a car park. Accounts visibly cool as they go untouched, which is the Champion Deposits cadence made honest. Per-account context pack for drafting the follow up.

When: before a call Cost: 2 min Pays back: next call
Strongest at
  • Sharpest immediate payoff of the three
  • Catches the promise you forgot, which is the expensive one
  • Closest to the Account Signal Monitor you specified
  • Load the example account to see it working
Honest risk
  • Overlaps whatever your CRM already claims to do
  • Heaviest to keep current across many accounts
  • Knowledge stays account shaped, so it generalises least
Open Account Brain

If you want one answer: start with V2, then add V3

The Dojo is the only one that is useful before you have put anything into it. That matters more than it sounds. V1 and V3 both ask you to invest for a fortnight before they give anything back, and the graveyard of personal knowledge tools is full of exactly that bargain. The Dojo pays out on the first rep, and it builds the same library sideways, as a by-product of getting sharper.

Then add Account Brain once the habit exists, because it has the sharpest single payoff: the promise you made nine days ago and have not sent. That is the one that actually costs you deals.

Field Notes is the purest expression of the gap and the best long term substrate. It is also the one most likely to be abandoned in March. Pick it only if you already know you are the sort of person who writes things down after a call.

05

What I did not do

I did not wire any of these to Gmail, the calendar, or a CRM, and I did not add anything that phones home. That is deliberate: the whole argument is that this store has to be yours, and the moment it depends on a connector it belongs to whoever owns the connector. All three export clean JSON and a markdown context pack, so the bridge to Claude is a paste rather than an integration.

The Dojo's deck is deliberately vendor neutral. Not one card asserts a product capability, a competitor gap or a statistic, because those go stale and because a practice tool that trains you to repeat an unverified claim is worse than no practice tool. Your own product specifics belong in your kept answers, where you can check them.

If you pick one

  1. Move the folder up to its own path, for example /dojo/, and add a card to the Tools section of the homepage.
  2. Give it a manifest.webmanifest and a service worker so it installs to your home screen and runs on a plane, the way Departures and Mediterranean OS already do.
  3. Delete the other two, and this page with them.
